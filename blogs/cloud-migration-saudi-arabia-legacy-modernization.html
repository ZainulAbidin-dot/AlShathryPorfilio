The Seven Types of Cloud Migration Strategies

Not all cloud migrations are created equal. Understanding different migration approaches helps you choose the strategy that matches your business needs and technical capabilities. Industry experts have identified seven distinct migration types, often called the "7 Rs":

  • Rehost (Lift and Shift): Moving applications to the cloud without modification. This approach offers the fastest migration but doesn't immediately leverage cloud-native benefits.
  • Replatform (Lift, Tinker, and Shift): Making minimal optimizations during migration without changing core architecture.
  • Repurchase (Drop and Shop): Moving to a different product, typically shifting from traditional licenses to SaaS models.
  • Refactor (Re-architect): Redesigning applications to be cloud-native, taking full advantage of cloud capabilities like serverless computing, microservices, and auto-scaling.
  • Retire: Identifying applications that are no longer needed.
  • Retain: Keeping certain applications on-premises due to compliance, latency, or other business requirements.
  • Relocate: Moving infrastructure to the cloud with minimal changes, often at the hypervisor level.

The key is matching strategy to each application's characteristics. Mission-critical systems might warrant refactoring, while less critical applications could be rehosted quickly.

The Five Phases of Successful Cloud Migration

Every successful cloud migration I've witnessed or led follows a similar pattern. These five phases create the roadmap from planning to optimization:

Phase 1: Assessment and Discovery

Before touching any infrastructure, you must understand what you have. Document every application, database, and integration. Assess dependencies between systems.

Phase 2: Strategic Planning

Define your cloud migration goals clearly. Are you reducing costs? Improving performance? Enabling new capabilities? Your goals determine your approach.

Phase 3: Design and Architecture

Design your target cloud environment. This includes network architecture, security controls, identity management, and disaster recovery plans.

Phase 4: Migration Execution

Execute migrations according to your prioritized roadmap. Start with pilot migrations to validate processes. Each migration should include testing phases, rollback plans, and success criteria.

Phase 5: Optimization and Management

After migration, the work continues. Optimize resource allocation, implement cost management practices, and leverage cloud-native features progressively.

Implementing the 5 R's Framework for Your Applications

The "5 R's" framework provides a practical decision-making tool for application migration. Here's how to apply it:

  • Rehost: Use this for applications where speed matters more than optimization.
  • Refactor: Reserve this for applications that are core to your competitive advantage.
  • Revise: Update applications with deprecated frameworks or unsupported databases.
  • Rebuild: Sometimes, rebuilding from scratch makes more sense than migrating technical debt.
  • Replace: Using SaaS alternatives often delivers better results at lower cost for common business functions.

The framework works best when applied systematically across your application portfolio, with decisions documented and reviewed by business stakeholders, not just IT.

Common Causes of Migration Challenges (And How to Avoid Them)

While the question "what are the four main causes of migration" often refers to human migration in general contexts, in cloud migration, four primary challenges consistently emerge:

  • Inadequate Planning: Rushing into migration without thorough assessment creates cascading problems.
  • Skills Gaps: Your team's expertise with on-premises infrastructure doesn't automatically translate to cloud proficiency.
  • Security Concerns: Misconceptions about cloud security cause hesitation. Work with professionals who understand cloud-native security models.
  • Cultural Resistance: People fear change. Address this through transparent communication.
